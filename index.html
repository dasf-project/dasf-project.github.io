Abstract

Ensuring collision avoidance while maintaining task progress remains a challenge for diffusion-based manipulation control. Constrained state-space plans remain sensitive to inverse-dynamics and tracking errors during execution, while path-preserving safety filters can stop unsafe motion but cannot redirect a blocked trajectory. To address these limitations, we propose a control barrier function (CBF) safety filter that enforces constraints directly within the action-denoising process of a pretrained diffusion policy. Our method combines trajectory-level projection under discrete-time CBF constraints with task-conditioned denoising to reshape nominal action chunks around obstacles without retraining the policy. At selected denoising steps, intermediate chunks are projected to satisfy CBF constraints and fed back into subsequent denoising steps. The controller executes a short action prefix and replans from updated observations. We evaluate our method on the Can, Square, and Lift tasks from robomimic and on a real UR3 manipulator. Across 300 simulation trials per method, our filter achieves an aggregate joint success-and-safety rate of 42.7%, exceeding the best constrained state-space diffusion baseline by 25.4 percentage points and path-preserving safety filtering by 32.4 percentage points. On Can and Square, denoising-time projection improves joint success and safety by up to 22.5 percentage points over final-output filtering, while adding obstacle-aware CBF constraints yields gains of up to 50.0 percentage points over action bounds alone. UR3 demonstrations further show obstacle-aware passage while the obstacle remains in the workspace.

Method

At every policy update, DASF samples a nominal action chunk from a frozen diffusion policy. During reverse diffusion, a sequential quadratic-program projection modifies the translational actions so that the predicted end-effector rollout satisfies discrete-time CBF conditions. Orientation and gripper commands remain unchanged. The projected sample is then passed back to the denoiser, coupling safety correction with the learned action prior.

Ablation Study

The Can and Square ablations compare projections applied during denoising with final-output filtering and action bounds alone. The results show that the improvement comes from incorporating obstacle-aware CBF constraints into the iterative denoising process rather than from clipping the generated actions.

Real-Robot Demonstration

We deploy the frozen diffusion policy and safety filters on a UR3 manipulator. The qualitative sequences contrast unfiltered execution and path-preserving braking with our denoising-time projection. Our method modifies the nominal chunk to navigate around the obstacle and completes the transport task without requiring the obstacle to be removed.
