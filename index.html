Denoising-Time Safety Filtering for Diffusion Policies via Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions

Anonymous Authors
ICRA 2027 Submission
▤Paper ▶Real-Robot Demo

Our method projects intermediate diffusion-policy action chunks onto discrete-time CBF constraints, reshaping nominal motion into an obstacle-aware trajectory while preserving task-directed behavior.

Abstract

Safe diffusion-based control requires enforcing collision-avoidance constraints without sacrificing task completion. Existing approaches either constrain diffusion-generated plans or apply safety filters during execution. Constraining a generated plan outside the policy can decouple safety correction from task-conditioned action generation, while execution-time filters that preserve the nominal path can halt unsafe motion but cannot actively redirect a blocked trajectory.

We introduce a discrete-time control barrier function safety filter that operates directly within the denoising process of a pretrained diffusion policy. Intermediate action chunks are sequentially projected onto trajectory-level safety constraints, and the corrected samples are returned to subsequent denoising steps. A short action prefix is executed before replanning from updated observations, enabling obstacle-aware modification without policy retraining.

Across 300 simulation trials per method on the Can, Square, and Lift tasks, our approach achieves the highest aggregate joint success-and-safety rate. Ablations support projection during denoising over final-output filtering and show that obstacle constraints improve safe task completion beyond action bounds alone. Experiments on a UR3 manipulator further demonstrate active obstacle avoidance while the obstacle remains in the workspace.

Method

At every policy update, DASF samples a nominal action chunk from a frozen diffusion policy. During reverse diffusion, a sequential quadratic-program projection modifies the translational actions so that the predicted end-effector rollout satisfies discrete-time CBF conditions. Orientation and gripper commands remain unchanged. The projected sample is then passed back to the denoiser, coupling safety correction with the learned action prior.

Ablation Study

The Can and Square ablations compare projections applied during denoising with final-output filtering and action bounds alone. The results show that the improvement comes from incorporating obstacle-aware CBF constraints into the iterative denoising process rather than from clipping the generated actions.

Real-Robot Demonstration

We deploy the frozen diffusion policy and safety filters on a UR3 manipulator. The qualitative sequences contrast unfiltered execution and path-preserving braking with our denoising-time projection. Our method modifies the nominal chunk to navigate around the obstacle and completes the transport task without requiring the obstacle to be removed.
